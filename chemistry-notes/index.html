1.1 Solids, Liquids and Gases • 1.2 Diffusion

CompressibilityVery lowVery lowHigh

State changes

  • Melting and boiling absorb heat energy
  • Freezing and condensation release heat energy
  • Pure substances have fixed melting and boiling points
  • Sublimation: solid to gas directly

Diffusion

Net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration. Faster when temperature is higher and particles are lighter.

2.1 Elements, Compounds and Mixtures • 2.2 Atomic Structure • 2.3 Isotopes • 2.4 Arrangement of Elements and Separation

Element

Made of one type of atom only.

Compound

Different elements chemically combined in fixed proportions.

Mixture

Substances physically mixed, not chemically joined, so they can be separated by physical methods.

Atomic structure

  • Proton: charge +1, mass 1
  • Neutron: charge 0, mass 1
  • Electron: charge -1, mass 1/1840
  • Atomic number = number of protons
  • Mass number = protons + neutrons

Isotopes

Atoms of the same element with the same proton number but different neutron numbers. Same chemical properties, different mass numbers.
Separation or arrangement pointWhat to remember
Arrangement of elementsElements are arranged in order of increasing proton number
PeriodsSame number of occupied electron shells
GroupsSame number of outer-shell electrons and similar chemical properties
FiltrationSeparates insoluble solid from liquid
CrystallisationObtains soluble solid from solution
Simple distillationObtains solvent or separates liquids with big boiling-point difference
Fractional distillationSeparates miscible liquids with close boiling points

8.2 Group I • 8.3 Group VII • 8.4 Transition Elements • 8.5 Noble Gases

TopicMust-know properties
Group ISoft metals, low density, one outer electron, react with water to form alkaline hydroxide and hydrogen, reactivity increases down group
Group VIIDiatomic non-metals, colored vapors, form halide ions, react with metals, reactivity decreases down group, more reactive halogen displaces less reactive halide
Transition elementsHigh melting point and density, good conductors, variable oxidation states, colored compounds, catalytic behavior
Noble gasesMonatomic, very unreactive, full outer shell, used in lamps and inert atmospheres

Group I pattern

Outer electron is lost easily, so reactivity increases down the group.

Group VII pattern

Atoms gain one electron to form -1 ions, and reactivity decreases down the group.

Displacement idea

Chlorine displaces bromide and iodide. Bromine displaces iodide. Iodine does not displace chloride or bromide.

2.5 Ionic Bonds • 2.5 Simple Molecules and Covalent Bonds • 2.6 Giant Covalent Structures • 2.7 Metallic Bonding

Ionic bonding

Electron transfer from metal to non-metal. Strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.

Simple covalent molecules

Non-metals share electrons. Low melting and boiling points because forces between molecules are weak.

Metallic bonding

Positive ions in a lattice with delocalised electrons. This explains conductivity and malleability.
Structure typeTypical propertiesExamples
Ionic latticeHigh melting point; conducts when molten or aqueous; brittleNaCl, MgO
Simple molecularLow melting point; poor conductorCO₂, CH₄, H₂O
Giant covalentVery high melting point; usually poor conductorDiamond, SiO₂
Metallic latticeGood conductor; malleable; ductileCu, Al, Fe

Graphite

Each carbon bonds to three others. One delocalised electron per atom, so it conducts electricity. Layers slide, so it is soft.

Diamond

Each carbon bonds to four others. Very hard, very high melting point, does not conduct.

3.1 Formulae, Equations and Ionic Equations • 3.2 Relative Masses of Atoms and Molecules

Formulae and equations

Learn how to write correct chemical formulae, balance symbol equations, and write ionic equations by removing spectator ions.

Relative masses

Mr is the sum of all relative atomic masses in a molecule or formula unit.
Relative formula mass Mr = sum of Ar values
Average atomic mass Ar = Σ(isotope mass × % abundance) / 100
Ionic equation tip Keep only ions that actually change
Balancing rule: total atoms and total charge must balance. Change coefficients, never subscripts.

7.1 Characteristic Properties of Acids and Bases • 7.2 Oxides

Definitions

Acid produces H⁺ in water. Base neutralises acid. Alkali is a soluble base producing OH⁻.

pH ideas

Low pH is acidic, 7 is neutral, high pH is alkaline.

Oxides

Metal oxides are usually basic. Non-metal oxides are usually acidic. Some, like Al₂O₃ and ZnO, are amphoteric.
Reaction patternProducts
Acid + metalSalt + hydrogen
Acid + baseSalt + water
Acid + carbonateSalt + water + carbon dioxide
Acid + ammoniaAmmonium salt

Salt names from acids

Hydrochloric acid gives chlorides, nitric acid gives nitrates, sulfuric acid gives sulfates, ethanoic acid gives ethanoates.

Preparation methods

Titration for soluble salts from alkalis. Add excess insoluble base or metal, then filter and crystallise, for many other soluble salts.

12.2 Acid-Base Titrations • 12.3 Experimental Design • 12.4 Chromatography

Titration relation c₁V₁ / n₁ = c₂V₂ / n₂

Concordant titres

Ignore the rough result and average titres that are close together, often within 0.10 cm³.

Experimental design

Identify variables, control conditions carefully, repeat readings, and suggest improvements for accuracy, precision, and safety.
Chromatography Rf = distance moved by substance / distance moved by solvent

Gas tests

  • Hydrogen: squeaky pop with lighted splint
  • Oxygen: relights glowing splint
  • Carbon dioxide: turns limewater milky
  • Chlorine: bleaches damp litmus paper
  • Ammonia: turns damp red litmus blue

Flame tests

Lithium crimson, sodium yellow, potassium lilac, calcium orange-red, copper blue-green.
